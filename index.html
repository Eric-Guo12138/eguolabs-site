The starting point

Technical enquiries rarely
arrive quote-ready.

For custom and application-led products, the first task is often not quoting — it is turning an incomplete customer message into something sales and engineering can actually review.

01

Incomplete requirements

Customers may know the application, but not every technical or commercial parameter.

02

Repeated clarification

Sales and engineering may need additional information before the right route becomes clear.

03

Messy handoffs

Requirements may be spread across email text, forms, drawings, BOMs and project notes.

04

Slow first response

The first useful response may wait until someone manually structures the enquiry.

From enquiry to clarity

before engineering review.

A few useful answers

Before we talk.

Does this replace an engineer?

No. It prepares the first pass — structuring the enquiry, identifying missing inputs and drafting the next response. Technical judgement stays with the team.

Do you need access to our CRM or production email?

Not for an initial pilot. Validation can start with anonymised historical enquiries.

Can it work with custom products rather than a fixed catalogue?

Yes. The output does not need to be a SKU match. It can identify a likely route, highlight missing requirements and prepare the enquiry for engineering review.

What do you need for a pilot?

A small set of representative historical enquiries, the fields or rules your team normally checks, and enough product or process information to model the first-pass workflow.

What about sensitive customer information?

Initial validation can use anonymised enquiries. Data handling and integration requirements should be agreed before any live deployment.
